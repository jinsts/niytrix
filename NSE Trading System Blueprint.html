01 · Overview

What this system is

You have a tested-by-hand price-action strategy but no time to sit in front of charts during market hours. This system watches selected NSE stocks, checks your entry rules every time a 15-minute candle closes, and places orders only when every rule agrees and every risk limit allows it. You observe through Telegram and a dashboard, and you spend your time on review rather than execution.

It is deliberately small. Earlier plans included local LLMs, Redis, TimescaleDB, a six-screen custom dashboard and a Rust engine. Most of that either already exists in OpenAlgo or isn't needed for a strategy that makes a few decisions a day.

Rules decide

Every entry and exit comes from code you can read and backtest. Nothing depends on a model's mood.

AI reviews, you approve

Claude reads the week's journal and proposes changes. No change reaches live trading until you accept it.

Reuse the plumbing

Broker connection, orders, paper trading, charts and alerts come from OpenAlgo. You build only what is yours.

Protection outlives crashes

Stops and loss limits sit at the broker or in OpenAlgo, so an open position stays covered if your service dies.

Two apps on one server. App A is OpenAlgo, run as published. App B is your service. P1 marks what is needed to go live; later phases are marked P2 and P3.

04 · Architecture

How the pieces fit

Everything that talks to Fyers runs on one EC2 instance, because Fyers only accepts orders from the static IP you registered. You manage it from anywhere over Tailscale.

Boundaries between the two apps

One order path

Only App B sends orders, and only through OpenAlgo's API. You place manual orders in OpenAlgo or the Fyers app, never both at once for the same symbol.

OpenAlgo is the truth

Positions and fills come from OpenAlgo's books. App B reconciles against them every cycle and never trusts its own copy.

Separate storage

App B keeps its own SQLite file. OpenAlgo upgrades can't touch your journal, and your code can't corrupt OpenAlgo's state.

A thin adapter

All OpenAlgo calls go through one small module in App B. If you ever need a direct Fyers fallback, only that module changes.

06 · Signal engine

The confluence gate

A trade needs all five of your conditions on the same closed candle. One missing condition means no trade.

Open question for you: how support and resistance are defined. Options include prior-day high/low, swing pivots over N candles, or opening-range levels. This needs to be fully mechanical before the backtest, because the backtest can only test what code can compute.
One code path

Rule functions take a candle table and return a decision. The backtest and live trading call the same functions, so they can't drift apart.

Closed candles only

Rules evaluate the candle that just closed, never a forming one. This avoids signals that flicker and vanish.

Stale data is a veto

If the latest candle is late or has gaps, the engine skips the cycle and says so.

07 · Risk model

Two layers of protection

App B decides whether a trade may happen. OpenAlgo and the broker make sure an open trade stays protected, even if App B stops.

Starting limits

These are placeholder defaults to make the design concrete. You set the real numbers after the backtest, and they belong in the config file. This isn't financial advice.

Usability rules

No edits during market hours

Config is read-only from 09:15 to 15:30. The kill switch is the one exception.

Two steps for anything destructive

The kill switch asks you to confirm with a second command before it acts.

Mode is always visible

Every message says SANDBOX or LIVE, so you never confuse paper trades with real ones.

Every alert says why

Entries list the conditions that passed. Skips name the rule that failed.

AI output is labelled

Review text carries the model name and date, and proposals are separate from facts.

Silence means healthy

A heartbeat runs every 15 minutes; you only hear about it when it misses.

10 · Weekly review

How the system learns

The learning loop is slow on purpose. One review a week, one set of decisions from you, and every change tested before it trades.

What the weekly pack contains

  • 1
    Win rate, average win and loss, expectancy, net P&L after costs
  • 2
    Results split by pattern, time of day, symbol and market trend
  • 3
    Skipped signals and what they would have done
  • 4
    Rule breaks, rejections, late data and other incidents
  • 5
    Your one-line daily notes

What a proposal looks like

Proposal 2 of 3 · Claude Sonnet · 25 OctRaise entry window start to 09:45 Evidence: 5 of 6 losses this month entered on the 09:30 candle. Change: entry_window.start 09:30 → 09:45 Backtest on 6 months: pending [Approve] [Reject] Example · illustrative
Guard against overfitting: only accept a change that holds on data the review didn't look at, and change one thing at a time. Weekly samples are small, so most proposals should be "watch this" rather than "change this".

12 · Compliance & security

Rules the system must follow

Retail algo framework (from April 2026)

  • Rule
    Static IPOrders only from a registered IP. One primary and one secondary are allowed, and changes are limited, so the bot stays on one EC2 Elastic IP.
  • Rule
    Daily login with 2FASessions don't carry over. You log in each morning.
  • Rule
    Order rateUnder 10 orders per second keeps a personal setup in the simplest category. This system places a handful a day.
  • Rule
    Market ordersConverted to market-price-protection orders by the broker. Prefer limit orders for entries.
  • Rule
    Kill switchRequired capability, and built in at both layers.

Confirm current details with Fyers before going live; broker implementations differ.

Security choices

  • Net
    Private by defaultDashboards are reachable only over Tailscale. No public ports except a webhook, later, if you add TradingView.
  • Keys
    Credentials in one placeFyers keys live only in OpenAlgo. App B holds an OpenAlgo API key and nothing else.
  • Login
    Manual 2FAYou log in by hand rather than storing your TOTP secret on the server for automation.
  • Code
    Pinned OpenAlgoUpgrade on weekends only, after sandbox testing.
  • Data
    Nightly backupsApp B's SQLite file and OpenAlgo's data snapshot to S3.
  • Licence
    AGPL awarenessFine for personal use. If this ever serves other people, keep your logic in App B and get the licensing checked.

14 · Roadmap

Build order, with gates

Each phase ends in a gate. You only move on when the gate passes.

Phase 0 · Prove the edge 1–2 weeks

  • Write the S/R definition and all rules as code
  • Backtest 1–2 years of 15-min data with costs
  • Check results by year, symbol and market regime
GatePositive expectancy after costs, on data you didn't tune on

Phase 1 · Plumbing in sandbox 1 week

  • EC2 with Elastic IP, Tailscale, OpenAlgo pinned
  • Register IP with Fyers; daily login works
  • Run the five checks in section 15
GateAll five checks pass, or a workaround is chosen

Phase 2 · App B, paper trading 2–3 weeks build, 4 weeks run

  • Signal engine, risk gate, adapter, journal, Telegram
  • Run end to end in sandbox for a month
  • Compare sandbox results with the backtest
GateSandbox matches backtest closely; zero unexplained mismatches

Phase 3 · Live, small 4+ weeks

  • Switch to live with minimum quantity
  • Add the screener, weekly review and versioned config
  • Scale size only after a stable month
GateA month inside your limits with no operational incidents

Phase 4 · Optional extras

  • Journal web page, TradingView webhooks, swing (CNC) trades
  • Add each only if you actually miss it

16 · Glossary

Terms used here

MIS
Intraday product; positions must close the same day.
CNC
Delivery product, used for swing trades held overnight.
S/R
Support and resistance price levels.
Confluence
Several independent conditions agreeing at once.
Strategy RMS
OpenAlgo's risk manager for per-strategy limits and stops.
Sandbox
OpenAlgo's paper-trading mode with live prices.
Historify
OpenAlgo's local store of historical candles.
Elastic IP
A fixed public IP on AWS, registered with Fyers.
MPP
Market price protection; market orders become capped limit orders.
Reconcile
Comparing your records with the broker's and fixing differences.
Expectancy
Average profit per trade after wins, losses and costs.
Kill switch
One action that cancels orders, closes positions and stops trading.
